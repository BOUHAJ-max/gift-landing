Notes on the Practice, Key Terms & a Glossary of the Inner World

The following notes are offered for readers who wish to situate the experiences described in this book within a more explicit technical framework. They are not a substitute for the serious study of the tradition — for that, the reader is directed to the bibliography. They are an orientation: a set of terms and their meanings as used in this specific work.

I. On the Transitional Phase

The transitional phase — known in the Western parapsychological literature as the hypnagogic state, and in the Islamic esoteric tradition as the barzakh of sleep — is the primary gateway to out-of-body experience and advanced lucid dreaming. It is characterized by the following phenomena, which appear with remarkable consistency across all practitioners regardless of cultural background.

First: Progressive muscular paralysis, beginning in the extremities and moving toward the core, produced by the brain's sleep-paralysis mechanism. In the ordinary sleeper, this paralysis is invisible because the sleeper is unconscious when it occurs. In the practitioner who maintains consciousness through the transition, it is fully perceptible and can be disorienting until its nature is understood.

Second: Auditory phenomena of high variety and intensity — engine sounds, electrical humming, voices, explosions, music. These sounds correspond to no physical acoustic event and are generated internally but experienced as fully external.

Third: Visual phenomena, ranging from geometric patterns and colors to fully formed scenes of remarkable detail. These appear with eyes closed and constitute the beginning of the visionary capacity that, fully developed, becomes the out-of-body perceptual system.

Fourth: Electrical or kinesthetic sensations — the experience of currents of energy moving through the body, the most reliable marker of the transitional phase, the signal that the second body is beginning its separation from the physical.

The appropriate response to the transitional phase is relaxed non-engagement: allowing the phenomena to proceed without either fighting them or following them into dream narratives. The practitioner maintains consciousness without directing it toward any specific content, attending to the quality of the state itself rather than to any particular element within it.

II. Key Terms from the Islamic Esoteric Tradition

Al-Batin al-Khayali — The imaginal inner world. The register of the inner world accessible through imagination and altered states. Characterized by responsiveness to the practitioner's own mental content: beliefs, expectations, fears, and desires all shape what is encountered here. Real, but not the deepest register of inner reality.

Al-Batin al-Haqiqi — The real inner world. The register of inner reality independent of the practitioner's mental content, that corrects rather than confirms existing beliefs. Accessible only through authentic religious practice and spiritual purification; not accessible through technique alone.

Al-Jism al-Lateef — The subtle body. Also called the etheric body in Western terminology. The second body capable of separation from the physical form and independent movement through space. Present in every human being; fully operative in the Abdal; partially operative through specific practices in ordinary practitioners.

Al-Insan al-Kamil — The Complete Human Being. The highest station available to the human being: the full and transparent embodiment of all the divine attributes in the specific configuration that constitutes this particular person. Not a type of person but a type of relationship — the relationship between the human being and the Real when all distances have been traversed.

Kashf — Unveiling. Genuine spiritual perception: the direct, non-mediated encounter with the inner reality of things, unfiltered by the practitioner's prior beliefs. Distinguished from khiyal (imagination) by its independence from the practitioner's mental content and by the quality and precision of the information it conveys.

Al-Ahwal — The spiritual states. The experiential conditions of genuine mystical development: bast (expansion), qabd (constriction), shawq (longing), wajd (finding), hayra (bewilderment), fana (annihilation), baqa (subsistence). Not emotions in the ordinary sense but experiences of a different order — amplified, total, and directly linked to the practitioner's relationship with the Real.

Sirr al-Harakat — The Secret of Movements. The title of the key Dar al-A'maq volume that provided Marudai with the framework for understanding the relationship between physical positions and invisible forces. Central thesis: every movement the human body makes attracts a specific category of energy, classifiable as either forces of light or forces of darkness.

Al-Abdal — The Substitutes. The forty spiritual poles of the Islamic cosmological hierarchy who maintain the world's equilibrium. Named the Substitutes because of their capacity to deploy the second body independently of the physical body, appearing simultaneously in multiple locations and producing visible material effects in the physical world.

III. On the Verification of Experiences

The out-of-body experience described in Chapter Six — the witnessing of a flash flood fifty kilometers from the practitioner's physical location, confirmed by telephone approximately one hour later — represents the clearest case in this record for an interaction between the astral state and objective physical reality.

The projection occurred on a Sunday morning in 2019, approximately nine in the morning. The practitioner had no prior knowledge of flood conditions in the target location and no access to weather information specific to that area. During the projection, the practitioner witnessed a flash flood arriving in the target wadi from the upstream direction — observed for approximately two to three minutes of subjective time before the physical body's recall initiated the return. The observations included the visual appearance of the flood front, the acoustic signature, and the general weather conditions at the target location.

Approximately one hour after waking, the practitioner contacted family members in the target village by telephone. They confirmed that a flash flood had arrived that morning, in the period corresponding to the projection, from the upstream direction the practitioner had observed. The details — timing, direction of approach, general scale — were consistent with what was witnessed during the projection.

The practitioner does not claim this constitutes scientific proof in the strict sense. He claims only that this experience, taken together with other documented cases in the parapsychological literature, constitutes evidence that warrants serious investigation and cannot be adequately explained by current mainstream psychological or neurological models.

IV. A Note on Religious Compliance

The practices described in this book occupy an ambiguous position within Islamic jurisprudence. They are not explicitly addressed in the primary sources in their contemporary form, though phenomena closely related to them — the bilocation of the Abdal, the spiritual ascent of the mystics, the visionary experiences of the prophets and saints — are extensively documented in the tradition.

The author does not endorse or encourage others to engage in these practices without proper religious guidance and a solid foundation in authentic Islamic practice. His own engagement with these practices is presented as historical — a period of his development that has since been closed — rather than as a current or recommended path. The practices are documented for research purposes and for the benefit of those who have already encountered these phenomena and require a framework for understanding them.

Readers who are experiencing the phenomena described in this book are encouraged to seek guidance from a qualified scholar of Islamic esoteric science rather than attempting to navigate the territory independently. The territory is real. The map is necessary. And the map is best read with a guide.

"The tradition is not an obstacle to experience. It is the accumulated wisdom of everyone who has had the experience before you and survived it with their understanding intact. Use it."

V. Recommended Reading

For those wishing to engage more deeply with the frameworks presented in this book, the author recommends beginning with the following, in roughly the order given.

The Dar al-A'maq series by El-Wakili and Abdelhaq, beginning with Sirr al-Harakat (The Secret of Movements) and Al-Ittisal al-Batini (The Inner Connection). These provide the most complete articulation of the religious esoteric framework that underlies the author's analysis.

Al-Risala al-Qushayriyya (The Qushayri Epistle), the most accessible classical introduction to the technical vocabulary and phenomenology of Sufi mystical development.

The works of Sheikh Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani, particularly Futuh al-Ghayb (Revelations of the Unseen), for the practical and ethical dimensions of the path.

For the Western parapsychological framework: Dean Radin's The Conscious Universe for the statistical evidence base; Pim van Lommel's Consciousness Beyond Life for the near-death experience research; Robert Monroe's Journeys Out of the Body for the phenomenology of deliberate out-of-body experience from a non-religious Western perspective.

And finally: the Quran itself, read with the quality of attention it deserves — not as cultural heritage, not as legal code, not as comfort literature, but as the direct address of the Real to the human being, which is what it is.
