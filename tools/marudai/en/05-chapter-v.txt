The Galaxy at His Feet

"He who has never left the Earth does not know what the Earth looks like. He has only ever seen it from inside. And inside is never the whole picture."

This was the projection he had been building toward since the beginning, though he had not known it until the moment it arrived. When it came, it came with a completeness and scale that left him, in the moment of its happening, in a state beyond language — the condition the Sufi tradition calls hayra, bewilderment, which is not confusion but the specific cognitive state that occurs when experience exceeds the mind's existing categories for understanding it.

The projection began as usual. The transitional phase and the subsequent separation. But from the first moment of being fully outside the body, there was a quality in the field that he recognized as different — an openness, an absence of the gentle gravitational pull toward familiar geography that had characterized his earlier journeys. As though the field itself had expanded, removed a ceiling he had not previously known was there.

He was standing in the open courtyard of their house in the village, looking up at the morning sky. The sky, as he always experienced it in the astral state, was not the atmospheric haze of physical perception but genuine deep space — the stars showing through the daytime brightness, the nearest astronomical objects identifiable by their positions in a field of view that was simultaneously intimate and infinite. He could see the mountains surrounding the village in perfect clarity — their ridgelines, their colors, the way they changed hue with altitude from the olive-green of the lower slopes to the ochre of the rock faces near the summit.

He formed the intention. Not as a verbal thought but as a directional wanting, a compass bearing aimed at infinity. He wanted to leave the Earth. To go up, and keep going, and see what there was to see.

"The universe is not empty space with occasional matter. It is full light with occasional shadow. Most humans live in the shadow and call it reality."

The acceleration was immediate and absolute. He did not experience a gradual gathering of speed; he was simply, in the next moment, moving upward at a velocity that made the concept of kilometers per hour meaningless. The mountains shrank to models, then to textures, then to the memory of mountains. The curvature of the Earth appeared — a detail so extraordinary that he would have stopped to contemplate it if stopping were possible, which it was not; the movement was its own momentum. The atmosphere thinned to a blue-white layer of improbable fragility, a film of air on a ball of rock, the only thing between all life and the void.

And then the void. The Earth below him, receding at a rate that quickly reduced it from a globe to a disc to a point of blue-white light among a field of other points. Around him, the stars — not as the flat ceiling of points that the naked physical eye perceives, but as three-dimensional objects at varying distances, each with its own color, its own brightness-gradient, its own position in a space that had genuine depth: foreground and middle ground and background, layer upon layer of light, extending in all directions without limit.

The speed increased. The stars around him began to show proper motion — the subtle shift in relative position that only becomes perceptible at enormous velocities, when the parallax of distance compresses and expands with the movement of the observer. He was moving through the galaxy. The stars clustered and thinned as different regions of the Milky Way passed through his field of awareness. He passed through a region so dense with stars that the sky around him was more bright than dark, the stars so numerous that the spaces between them were only gaps in a fabric of light. And then the density decreased, and he was in a quieter region, and the galaxy behind him had become a faint smear of brightness in the background.

The Rings

The expansion of scale continued. The galaxy receded. Other galaxies appeared — island universes, each containing billions of stars, each a point of light to his now cosmological perspective. They clustered as galaxies cluster, into filaments and walls and voids that structured the observable universe like the foam of some incomprehensible soap bubble.

And then he saw something he has never been able to adequately explain. In the distance — and distance, at this scale, is almost meaningless as a descriptor — he saw a ring. A circular structure of light, of enormous scale, surrounding the point that represented everything he had traveled from. As he continued outward, he saw more rings — each larger, each concentric with the last, each surrounding all that came before it, the way the rings of a tree surround the heartwood, the way the heavens of tradition are described as surrounding one another in a structure of nested, concentric spheres.

He was pulled toward one of these rings. And as he passed through it — or arrived at the place where it was, which may not be the same thing — he found himself on a surface. A world, with geography: red hills of a color that had no name in any language he knew, a rust-colored soil that smelled of iron and deep time. At its center stood a strange tree — tall as a tower, its leaves not spreading outward from the crown in the way of any tree he knew, but descending from the top, covering the trunk completely in a cascade of foliage that moved in a wind he could not feel. The sky above this world displayed the rings he had been traveling among — visible from the surface as enormous concentric circles of light, filling the entire sky like a cosmic mandala, each ring impossibly vast, each one still.

He sat on a rock on that world, in that alien and beautiful place, and he felt entirely at peace. Not the peace of absence of stimulus, but the peace of complete presence. The peace that the tradition calls sakina — divine tranquility — which descends on the heart that has surrendered completely to what is, without reservation.

Then the body's recall began. And he was pulled back through the distances in a matter of seconds. And he woke on the bed, in the village, with the ordinary sky through the window, and he lay for a very long time without moving or speaking, in the knowledge that something had shifted permanently in his understanding of what the word universe meant.
