The Complete Human Being

"Al-Insan al-Kamil — the Complete Human Being — is not a destination. It is a direction. No one arrives. Everyone, in the tradition's most honest moments, is described as being on the way."

The concept that organizes everything in the Islamic mystical tradition — the concept Marudai has returned to, circled, departed from, and returned to again across the full arc of his development — is al-insan al-kamil: the Complete Human Being. It is not, in the tradition's deepest formulations, a description of a type of person. It is a description of a type of relationship — the relationship between the human being and the Real when all the distances have been traversed and all the veils have been removed and what remains is the human being as the fullest and most transparent possible expression of the divine in the form of a human being.

This is what the ninety-nine names of God mean, in this framework. Not a list of attributes. A map of qualities that the human being is designed to embody — each name a possibility, each possibility a direction, and the full embodiment of all of them simultaneously the definition of completeness. Mercy and Justice. Power and Gentleness. The Apparent and the Hidden. The Complete Human Being is not the one who has chosen one of these and maximized it. The Complete Human Being is the one in whom all of them have found their proper proportion, their proper relationship, their proper expression in the specific and unrepeatable configuration that constitutes this particular human being in this particular existence.

"You are not on the way to becoming something you are not. You are on the way to becoming what you already are, fully. This is harder than becoming something new. It requires you to stop pretending to be less than you are."

The practical implications are demanding. They ask not for the addition of spiritual knowledge on top of an unchanged ordinary life, but for the transformation of the ordinary life itself — for the progressive extension of the quality of consciousness present in the best moments of genuine prayer or genuine love or genuine creative work into all the moments of the day, including the moments of tiredness, frustration, boredom, and the specific irritation of ordinary human interaction with ordinary human difficulty.

This is the practice. Not the out-of-body travel, not the lucid dreams, not the conversations with sages in the coffee houses of ecstatics, not the voyage to the edge of the universe. Those are experiences. Valuable experiences, experiences that changed the landscape of the possible for him permanently. But experiences are not practice. Practice is what you do every day, in the ordinary conditions of the ordinary life, to become what the tradition says it is possible to become.

He prays five times. He reads. He attempts to treat every human being he encounters as someone made in the image of the Real — which means treating them with the specific quality of serious attention that the genuinely sacred deserves. He does not always succeed. On the days when he fails, he begins again. The tradition does not ask for perfect performance. It asks for the direction, consistently maintained through all the falls and recoveries, pointed toward what is genuinely true and genuinely good and genuinely real.

"The Complete Human Being is not the one who has arrived. It is the one who has stopped pretending that the journey is anything other than what it is: the whole point."

What the Sea Cannot Hold

by Mohamed Bouhaj

Who said that love extinguishes our light —

has climbed the throne of hollow pride too fast.

I will retrain your eye to see aright,

and school your soul in torment unsurpassed.

Now look: your heart — a lantern almost spent.

Look at my heart: a fire that will not fall.

For all the oceans I have not been bent,

the sea cannot contain what I would call.

I dedicate the fullness of each wave

to speak my love for the Beloved, whole —

no poet's measure was ever made to save

the weight of this upon a mortal scroll.

So let the old forms rest; they cannot hold

the word that burns too brightly to be told.

The ships sail where the winds do not wish —

and I am the wind.

I am also the ship.

I am also the sea that neither can outrun.

Choose which one you want to argue with.

I will still be here when the argument is done.
